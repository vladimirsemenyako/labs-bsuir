Long benchmark text 5: A brief history of music.

The history of music in Western culture stretches across many centuries. Early musical traditions were closely connected with religion, ritual, and everyday work. In medieval Europe monks developed a system of written notation for sacred chants. This innovation allowed composers to fix melodies on paper and to share them between monasteries and cities. Over time, simple monophonic chants evolved into more complex polyphonic structures. Several independent voices could sound together, creating rich harmonies that filled cathedrals and churches.

During the Renaissance period music gradually left the exclusive space of the church and entered courts and public theatres. Composers wrote secular songs, instrumental dances, and the first experiments that later led to opera. New instruments such as the lute, the viol, and the harpsichord expanded the palette of available sounds. In the Baroque era the orchestra and the opera house became central institutions of musical life. Composers learned to write for larger ensembles and to organise music in multi-movement forms such as the concerto and the suite.

The Classical and Romantic periods introduced further changes. The piano replaced the harpsichord as the main keyboard instrument, and the symphony became a key genre for public concerts. Composers like Mozart, Beethoven, and Brahms explored contrast, thematic development, and dynamic range in new ways. Later in the nineteenth century music became more expressive and personal. Composers experimented with harmony and colour and sometimes used folk melodies to express national identity. In the twentieth century no single style dominated. Classical traditions coexisted with jazz, rock, electronic music, and many other genres. This long benchmark text is intended to simulate a relatively large document in the experimental corpus.
